Project datasets

Choose one of these six datasets, or propose your own (email the instructor early). Each one is well known, and each has its own specifics and problems your design has to deal with.

Each section links to the original data. You choose the subset your system works with, and say which in section 4 of your proposal. For five of the datasets, we give you a small sample, the preview, to speed up your data exploration for the proposal. It is in the course bucket s3://436c-2026w1-project-data/preview/, one folder per dataset: ghcn/, synpuf/, openalex/, maps/ and food/. Your sandbox can read it once your team lease arrives. Until then, explore the original data, which needs no AWS account. For GDELT, you read the live feed itself.

Your live updates are generated from your answers to the data questions in your proposal. The dataset itself is your history.

Explore the original data

DuckDB reads Parquet and CSV files straight from S3, so you can count, filter and sample a dataset without downloading it. The original datasets are in public buckets, so you need no AWS account for this. This query counts the days of each kind of observation at Vancouver International Airport:

INSTALL httpfs; LOAD httpfs;
SET s3_region = 'us-east-1';

SELECT ELEMENT, count(*) AS days, min(DATE) AS first_day, max(DATE) AS last_day
FROM read_parquet('s3://noaa-ghcn-pds/parquet/by_station/STATION=CAN01108395/*/*.parquet',
                  hive_partitioning = true)
GROUP BY ELEMENT
ORDER BY days DESC;

It reads only that station’s files, and it takes a few seconds. To explore a dataset:

  1. Start with one partition or one file, and run DESCRIBE and SUMMARIZE on it to see the columns.
  2. Filter on the partition columns in the path, so DuckDB reads only the files it needs.
  3. Count rows and measure size for the subset you have in mind. Those numbers go into your proposal.
  4. Set s3_region to the region of the bucket: us-east-1 for most of these, us-west-2 for Overture.

DuckDB also reads files over HTTPS, and from Hugging Face with hf:// paths, which covers the Open Food Facts file and the City of Vancouver exports. For OpenAlex, the API is often quicker than the snapshot.

NOAA GHCN-Daily weather

What it is. Daily weather observations from stations around the world: temperature, precipitation, snow and more. Registry page. Licence: CC0.

What is in it.

  • One row per station, day and element, with quality flags.
  • Station metadata in fixed-width text files.
  • Updated daily, about two days behind.
  • There are 9,906 Canadian stations.
  • Canadian station IDs start with CAN0. The ID without CAN0 is the station’s Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC) climate ID.

Original data. s3://noaa-ghcn-pds/parquet/by_station/ and by_year/ (us-east-1), back to the 1800s.

Preview. Canada from 1 January 2015, partitioned by year: 3,055 stations with temperature, precipitation or snow, and the station list.

What to design for.

  • The data is daily, but your live updates arrive every few minutes. Your system has to roll them up into days.
  • The two layouts in the bucket, by station and by year, serve different questions. The by-year files cannot skip stations outside Canada.
  • There is no text, so the generative-AI-first shape has to answer from numbers.

Good additions. ECCC’s station list (province, elevation) joins on the ID without CAN0. ECCC’s hourly data gives real updates below one day.

CMS synthetic patients (OMOP)

What it is. Synthetic Medicare patients built to look like real claims data, converted to the OMOP common data model. Registry page. Public use, synthetic.

What is in it.

  • The 100,000-person set: 15 OMOP tables (persons, visits, conditions, drugs, procedures and more).
  • About 0.9 GB of gzipped CSV, with header rows.
  • Visits from 2008 to 2010.

Original data. s3://synpuf-omop/ (us-east-1), gzipped CSV in three sizes: 1,000, 100,000 and 2.3 million persons.

Preview. The 100,000-person set as Parquet, with a label table for ICD-9 diagnosis and procedure codes. The bucket has no OMOP vocabulary, so codes are numbers until you join labels.

What to design for.

  • Diagnoses come as ICD-9-CM codes. The label table names 99% of diagnosis rows.
  • Procedures are labelled for only 37% of rows. Most of the rest are CPT-4 codes, which have no free label file.
  • Drug codes (NDC) can be labelled only in part.
  • The data is static, so your live updates (admissions, discharges, prescriptions) are generated.
  • There is no free text.

Good additions. The CMS ICD-9-CM description files, for diagnosis names. The FDA NDC directory, for drug names.

OpenAlex scholarly works

What it is. An open catalogue of scholarly works, authors, institutions, sources, funders and topics. openalex.org. Licence: CC0.

What is in it.

  • About 465 million works.
  • Each work carries topics, its publication year, references, authorships, and an abstract.
  • The abstract is stored as a word index, not as plain text.

Original data. The snapshot in s3://openalex/data/parquet/ (us-east-1), and the OpenAlex API.

Preview. One topic, 2015 to 2025. For Cloud Computing and Resource Management, that is 86,024 works as the primary topic. We rebuilt the abstract as text for 64,241 of them (75%).

What to design for.

  • The full snapshot is very large and is not partitioned by topic or year. Do not scan all of it: read a few of its files, or use the API.
  • Abstracts need rebuilding from the word index before you can use them as text.
  • Citations arrive over time, so “how cited is this work” keeps changing.

Good additions. ROR, the registry of research organisations (CC0), joins on each institution’s ROR ID. NSERC and CIHR award lists join by name, and only roughly.

Vancouver maps: Overture places and city data

What it is. Places from Overture Maps, and City of Vancouver open data. Licences: Overture places CDLA-Permissive-2.0; City of Vancouver data, the Open Government Licence – Vancouver.

What is in it.

  • 46,038 Overture places inside a Vancouver bounding box, about 5 MB.
  • The city’s 22 local area boundaries.
  • 207,243 business licences.

Original data. Overture releases in s3://overturemaps-us-west-2/release/ (us-west-2), and the City of Vancouver open data portal.

Preview. These three as Parquet files.

What to design for.

  • Questions are about places and areas, so you need a spatial key, such as a grid cell or a local area. Spatial queries were not taught.
  • Places open, close and get edited, so their current state changes.
  • Overture lives in a different AWS region from your sandbox.

Good additions.

  • City of Vancouver Open Data: business licences, street trees, local area boundaries.
  • TransLink GTFS schedules. GTFS-realtime is a real live feed and needs a free key.
  • Statistics Canada census areas.

Open Food Facts

What it is. A crowd-sourced catalogue of food products: names, ingredients, categories, nutrition. world.openfoodfacts.org. Licence: ODbL.

What is in it.

  • About 4.9 million products.
  • Canadian products are well filled in: names about 97%, categories about 89%, ingredients about 71%.

Original data. The product database on Hugging Face (food.parquet, with ingredients text), Open Prices on Hugging Face (prices.parquet), and Statistics Canada table 18-10-0245-01.

Preview. 124,863 Canadian products, without ingredients text, and Statistics Canada monthly average retail prices.

What to design for.

  • Assignment 3 already joined prices to receipts, and Assignment 4 built catalogue search. Your project needs a different question. Assignment 3 already used French products and prices and reported the share of spend on Nutri-Score A and B products. Your project needs a question that Assignment 3 did not answer.
  • Open Food Facts’ own price data has almost nothing for Canada, so prices need another source. Open Prices, the price data Open Food Facts collects, has 714 Canadian records. If you want to use Canadian data, take prices from Statistics Canada’s monthly average retail prices. They are averages by product type, not prices of single products, so you map product categories to those types yourself; they do not join by barcode. Otherwise, choose a country that Open Prices covers well.
  • France has about 220,000 Open Prices records for about 79,000 products at about 2,500 locations (November 2026).
  • The preview holds Canadian products only, so if you want to use French data instead, take French products from the original data.
  • Products are edited over time, so the catalogue has a current state and a history.

Good additions.

  • Statistics Canada monthly average retail prices (table 18-10-0245), by product type.
  • USDA FoodData Central branded foods, by barcode.
  • Open Food Facts’ category and ingredient taxonomies.

GDELT 2.0 world news events

What it is. World news coded into events (who did what to whom, where, with what tone), updated every 15 minutes. gdeltproject.org. Terms: free and unrestricted use.

What is in it. Every 15 minutes, three files:

  • events: 1,000 to 2,600 rows;
  • mentions: 2,400 to 4,100 rows;
  • GKG, themes and sources: 34 to 70 times the size of the events file.

Original data and preview. No preview: you read the live feed, with the course’s GDELT pipeline from the lectures as a recipe. The course bucket has the CAMEO code and country lookup tables in preview/gdelt/. Your test runs still use generated updates of the same shape, so the checks can compare against what really happened.

What to design for.

  • This is the only real live feed on the list.
  • Mentions of an event arrive late, a median of an hour after the event, so late data is built in.
  • The same article URL appears in many rows.
  • A listed file is sometimes missing, so your pipeline has to tolerate gaps.
  • Leave out the GKG, or take a small part of it: it is almost all of the volume.
  • Article text cannot be redistributed.
